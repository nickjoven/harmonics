# Klein-Z_2 third-caveat test — does substrate populate both R-eigenstates?

## Status

**Caveat outcome: SPLIT VERDICT.** The third caveat from
`klein_z2_decomposition_falsifier.md` — "whether substrate
dynamics actually populates both R-eigenstate components, or
only one" — resolves into two non-equivalent readings of
"populates," and the framework's apparatus passes one cleanly
and fails the other in a way that is itself informative.

- **Modal reading** (PASSES): Are there substrate modes in both
  R-even and R-odd sub-spectra? Yes. `klein_bottle.md` L118-134
  explicitly carries cos(2πny/L_y) and sin(2πny/L_y) y-modes,
  paired complementarily with antiperiodic-x and periodic-x mode
  types by the XOR rule `p_x + p_y ≡ 1 (mod 2)`.
- **Generative reading** (FAILS): Is there an independent
  R-symmetry generator on the substrate that can act as a
  chirality-flipper? No. `complex_amplitude_uniqueness.md`
  L94-124 derives **exactly one** complex structure J on the
  substrate, corresponding to the single antiperiodic (x) cycle.
  R acts only as the base half of the single coupled (S × R)
  Klein-bottle identification; there is no independent R-only
  cycle.

This split refines the falsifier verdict further. The audit and
first falsifier loaded the term "chirality" — borrowed from
particle physics — onto a substrate that operates on a different
axis: **y-parity**, the (cos, sin) eigenstate split of the
periodic y-direction, paired by XOR with x-mode type. Whether
substrate y-parity maps onto observed particle chirality is a
separate, downstream derivation question the framework has not
addressed by name.

Class: foundational rigor check (Class 3, second-order falsifier
test).

## The two readings of "populates"

### Modal reading — sub-spectrum population

`klein_bottle.md` L107-134: under the Klein-bottle identification

    f(x + L_x, y) = -f(x, L_y - y)

separation `f(x, y) = φ_m(x) ψ_n(y)` requires consistency:

    φ_m(x + L_x) × ψ_n(L_y - y) = -φ_m(x) × ψ_n(y)

The y-modes split into R-eigenstates:

| y-mode | R-eigenvalue | Paired with | x-mode wavenumbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| cos(2πny/L_y), n > 0 | +1 (R-even) | antiperiodic-x | (2k+1)π/L_x |
| sin(2πny/L_y), n > 0 | −1 (R-odd) | periodic-x | 2kπ/L_x |
| constant (n = 0) | +1 (trivially) | antiperiodic-x | (2k+1)π/L_x |

Both R-eigenvalues appear in the populated spectrum. The XOR
rule `p_x + p_y ≡ 1 (mod 2)` forbids the diagonal pairings
(even-x × even-y, odd-x × odd-y) but explicitly admits the
off-diagonal pairings (even-x × odd-y, odd-x × even-y) — one of
each is R-even, one is R-odd.

**Verdict**: substrate populates both R-eigenstates. Modal reading
PASSES.

### Generative reading — independent R-action

`complex_amplitude_uniqueness.md` L75-124 derives the substrate's
complex-structure operator J:

- The only source of J with J² = −I is an antiperiodic cycle.
- The Klein bottle has **exactly one** antiperiodic cycle (the
  x-cycle; the y-cycle is periodic).
- Periodic cycles contribute nothing (e^{i k_y L_y} = +1).
- Therefore exactly one J — the coupled (S × R) operator
  associated with x-half-loop.
- Quaternionic structure ℍ is *excluded* because it would require
  three independent antiperiodic cycles; the substrate has one.

The same reasoning rules out an independent R-only generator:
such a generator would arise from a second antiperiodic cycle
acting on the base manifold only, and the Klein bottle does not
have a second antiperiodic cycle. The R-action is **available
only as the base half of the single (S × R) coupled cycle**;
it is not separately generated by the substrate.

**Verdict**: substrate has no independent R-action generator.
Generative reading FAILS.

## Why the split matters

The two readings diverge on what kind of work the R-eigenstate
distinction can do downstream:

- **Modal sufficiency is enough** for dissipation profiles that
  act asymmetrically on the two sub-spectra. If horn-branch
  regime-change dissipation needs the substrate to have two
  populated parity sectors so it can dissipate them
  asymmetrically, modal sufficiency provides that. The cos-y and
  sin-y sectors are populated *and* live on different x-mode
  ladders (antiperiodic vs periodic wavenumbers), so a regime
  change can naturally treat them differently. **Task 105
  (horn-branch) is unblocked at the apparatus level.**

- **Modal sufficiency is not enough** for a substrate-level
  chirality-flipping operator that exchanges R-eigenstates
  independent of S. Any operation on the substrate that flips R
  is forced to flip S as well (they live on the same single J).
  Complex conjugation `J → −J` is the only available
  handedness-flip, and it flips the *coupled* (S × R) together.
  **A standalone chirality-extension that adds an independent
  R-generator would require introducing a second antiperiodic
  cycle, which is exactly the structural extension the audit
  identified.**

The framework's apparatus, in other words, has a **populated R-parity
spectrum** but **not an R-acting symmetry**. These are two
different things, and conflating them is what the first
falsifier's "APPARATUS ASYMMETRICALLY SUFFICIENT" verdict was
implicitly doing.

## Vocabulary correction — "chirality" is not framework-native

The audit and first falsifier both used "chirality" as if it were
the framework's vocabulary for the R-eigenstate distinction. Three
pieces of evidence say it is not:

1. **Not in `canonical_glossary.md`.** The framework has explicit
   vocabulary for handedness-adjacent concepts (sector, generation,
   parity, twist, sign) but no entry for chirality.

2. **`mass_sector_closure.md` L46-65 rejects the chirality reading
   of k_lepton = q_3².** The derivation explicitly considers
   "chiral copies × adj dim = 2 × 3 = 6" and writes "No. The
   formula is *squared*, not doubled." The actual mechanism is
   walker round-trip ("traverse the adjoint twice — once to reach
   the doublet partner, once to return"). The "chirality structure"
   language at L121-123 is descriptive paraphrase, not the
   derivation engine.

3. **`klein_bottle.md`'s native vocabulary is parity, not
   chirality.** L141-152 gives the selection rule in terms of
   (p_x, p_y) ∈ {0, 1}², and the XOR rule lives there. "Reflection"
   appears as a geometric operation (line 77, 83) but never as
   an eigenvalue split of the field that the framework would call
   "chirality."

The framework-native concept is therefore **y-parity** (or more
precisely, the R-eigenvalue ±1 of the y-mode under the y-reflection
that is the base half of the single coupled (S × R)
identification). The substrate has y-parity natively. Whether
substrate y-parity is *the same thing as* particle chirality —
the γ⁵ eigenvalue of a Dirac spinor — is a separate downstream
question that the framework has not addressed.

This may be a real open question. The substrate is scalar-phased,
not spinor-valued; the complex structure J that gives `J² = −I`
on half-integer x-modes is built from an antiperiodic scalar
cycle, not a γ-matrix structure. The phenomenological identification
"substrate y-parity ↔ particle chirality" is one the framework
has not yet derived (nor, importantly, has it ruled out).

## Implications for pending tasks

### Task 105 (horn-branch regime-change for Class B/C residuals)

**Status: UNBLOCKED at apparatus level.** The horn-branch's
dissipation-under-regime-change story can proceed because both
y-parity sectors are populated and live on different x-mode
ladders. Asymmetric regime-change dissipation does not require
an independent R-generator — it only requires populated sectors
to dissipate asymmetrically into.

What the horn-branch *cannot* assume: that any of its outputs
constitute a substrate-level chirality assignment. If horn-branch
work produces a mass for the muon at 37%-or-better resolution,
that derivation will be framed in terms of which y-parity sector
the dissipation lands on, not in terms of γ⁵ eigenstates.

### Task 110 (chirality-extension iteration arc)

**Status: REDIRECTED, not cancelled.** Two distinct possible
futures for this task:

- **(a) Vocabulary-bridge work.** Verify whether the framework's
  y-parity decomposition can be *identified with* observed
  particle chirality through some derivation chain (e.g., showing
  that the y-parity sector that horn-branch dissipates produces
  the observed left-handed weak coupling). This is downstream
  derivation work, not apparatus extension.

- **(b) Second antiperiodic cycle (apparatus extension).** Only
  needed if (a) fails — if it turns out that substrate y-parity
  *cannot* be identified with particle chirality. Then a second
  independent antiperiodic cycle would be the structural
  extension required, with the corresponding restructure of the
  substrate's complex-structure count (potentially admitting ℍ
  rather than ℂ, with downstream consequences for the Born rule
  derivation).

The (a)/(b) branch order matters: pursue (a) first, since (b)
would have large downstream consequences requiring re-derivation
of multiple closed framework results (Born rule, Tsirelson bound,
the ℂ-uniqueness theorem itself).

### Task 107 (audit) — disposition update

The audit's "APPARATUS INSUFFICIENT" verdict was correct in the
generative reading and incorrect in the modal reading. The
audit should be updated to reflect the split:

> APPARATUS HAS MODAL Y-PARITY (cos/sin sectors populated, XOR-paired
> with x-mode types). APPARATUS LACKS INDEPENDENT R-GENERATOR (only
> one J, the coupled S × R). For dissipation-style derivations the
> apparatus is sufficient; for symmetry-flipping-style derivations
> the apparatus is insufficient and would require a second
> independent antiperiodic cycle.

The original audit's three named extension paths (V_4 promotion,
cascade-attached Z_2, Klein-bottle restructure) now have a
distinguished ordering: V_4 promotion and cascade-attached Z_2
are vocabulary/labeling work over the existing apparatus;
Klein-bottle restructure (adding a second antiperiodic cycle)
is the only genuine apparatus extension among them.

## Falsifier-of-the-falsifier-of-the-falsifier — caveats

This test introduces its own falsification surface. Three points
where this verdict could itself be wrong:

1. **The XOR rule's R-eigenvalue interpretation.** The mode table
   in `klein_bottle.md` L130-134 says cos-y pairs with
   antiperiodic-x and sin-y pairs with periodic-x. The
   identification of cos as R-even and sin as R-odd is standard,
   but the *dynamical* claim — that substrate sync-cost evolution
   actually populates both classes under generic initial conditions
   — has not been verified against simulation data. If sync-cost
   minimization on the Klein bottle preferentially selects only
   one of the two parity sectors, the modal reading would
   collapse to the generative reading's verdict (apparatus
   insufficient).

2. **The y-parity ↔ chirality identification.** Even if both
   y-parity sectors are populated, mapping them onto observed
   particle chirality requires showing they couple asymmetrically
   to weak gauge bosons (or whatever apparatus produces the
   parity-violating SM phenomenology). The framework has not done
   this derivation. The vocabulary correction ("y-parity, not
   chirality") is honest but it surfaces this as an open
   item.

3. **The Klein-Z_2 restructure path's framework cost.** Adding a
   second antiperiodic cycle, if it turns out to be needed, would
   move the substrate from one J to two anticommuting J's, which
   in `complex_amplitude_uniqueness.md`'s framework selects ℍ
   (quaternionic) rather than ℂ. This would invalidate the
   uniqueness theorem for ℂ-amplitudes, the Born exponent 2, and
   the Tsirelson bound — all of which the framework currently
   treats as closed. Before committing to this path, the
   downstream re-derivations must be scoped.

## One-line summary

The third caveat of `klein_z2_decomposition_falsifier.md` splits
into a modal reading (passes — substrate populates both cos-y
and sin-y sub-spectra via XOR pairing with x-mode types) and a
generative reading (fails — substrate has exactly one J = (S × R)
coupled operator, no independent R-generator), and the
distinction reveals that the audit and first falsifier loaded
"chirality" as imported vocabulary onto a substrate whose native
concept is y-parity; Task 105 (horn-branch) is unblocked at
apparatus level since modal sufficiency provides populated
sectors for asymmetric dissipation, while Task 110
(chirality-extension) is redirected to vocabulary-bridge work
(showing substrate y-parity maps onto observed particle
chirality) with apparatus extension (adding a second antiperiodic
cycle) held as fallback only if the vocabulary-bridge fails — a
fallback whose framework cost includes invalidating the
ℂ-uniqueness theorem and downstream Born/Tsirelson results.
