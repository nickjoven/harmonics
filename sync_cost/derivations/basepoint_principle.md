# The Basepoint Principle

> **READ FIRST — what this principle is and is not.** A first-class
> framework principle (peer to the no-rescaling principle and the
> `substrate_determinism.md` inviolables), elevating the
> multiply-verified "structurally-forced decline" meta-pattern.
> **Its heart is a discriminator, not a licence.** It is **NOT**
> "anything the framework cannot derive is a structural feature."
> It applies *only* with a demonstrated forcing obstruction;
> absent that, the item is genuinely open. Misreading this as a
> blanket excuse to stop deriving is the one failure mode it
> exists to prevent.

## Statement

**The framework supplies torsorial structure — relations, ratios,
discrete counts, finite matrix elements — and never the
basepoints. Every basepoint the structure cannot canonically
select is *correctly declined*; and a decline is a structural
feature *iff* the absence of a selecting section is **structurally
forced** (an inviolable or a number-theoretic obstruction proves
no substrate route exists), not merely operational (no obstruction
proven — just unsearched).**

A *torsor* is a structure with a free transitive symmetry and **no
distinguished element** ("a group that forgot its identity"). The
framework's content is torsor-*invariant*: well-defined without any
basepoint. A basepoint is what a torsor structurally lacks; the
framework's honest stance is to supply the torsor and decline the
basepoint — with the discriminator deciding whether that decline
is a *feature* or an *open item*.

## The discriminator (the operative core — never omit it)

| Absence of a selecting section is… | …then the item is |
|---|---|
| **structurally forced** — an inviolable, a no-equivariant-section lemma, or a number-theoretic obstruction *proves* no substrate route can exist | a **structural feature** (correctly declined; closed) |
| **merely operational** — no such obstruction is proven; the section may simply be unfound | **genuinely open** (stays open; keep deriving) |

The principle is invoked **only** in the first row, **only** with
the obstruction exhibited. The second row is the guard: it keeps
the framework honest (cf. the documented honest-landing-loop /
Class-2 discipline).

## Canonical instance: the dimensional inputs

The two dimensionful anchors are the principle's clearest worked
case and fix its metaphysical content:

- **Nature.** A dimensionful scale is an **ℝ₊-torsor** (ℝ₊ acts
  freely/transitively on unit choices; no canonical unit). The
  substrate produces only torsor-invariant quantities
  (dimensionless ratios). An anchor is the basepoint the torsor
  cannot supply from within — the continuous-scale analog of ∅'s
  missing Z₂-torsor basepoint. The anchors are **not substrate
  entities**; they are *unforced relational pegs*, the same
  ontological category as the ∅ root posit (`substrate_determinism.md`'s
  "a reading of *what is*, not *why*", at the level of scale).
- **Dynamics involved: none, necessarily, at the point of input.**
  Every substrate dynamic (the tick, Kuramoto locking, the
  Schwinger relation, epoch transitions) is torsor-invariant —
  it runs identically regardless of basepoint. The anchor is
  dynamically inert by the same necessity that makes ∅ the unique
  *undecorated* fork (no feedback / destructive interface /
  epoch). All dynamics are intra-torsor (ratio-level); fixing the
  scale adds a *reading*, not a dynamic.
- **Why exactly two — forced.** `#ANCHOR`'s rigorous #5: the K=1
  (cosmological) and K<1 (particle) sectors are non-smoothly
  decoupled. Two decoupled sectors ⇒ two independent
  scale-torsors ⇒ exactly two basepoints, which cannot share.
- **Value — correctly declined.** A torsor has no canonical
  section; `#ANCHOR` established this is a feature, not a gap.

So the framework *can* describe the dimensional inputs'
**category** (unforced relational peg), **number** (two, forced by
sector decoupling), and **dynamics** (none, by torsor-invariance)
— and correctly **declines their values**. That asymmetry
(nature/number sayable; value declined) *is* the Basepoint
Principle.

## Bright line (load-bearing — do not over-read)

This principle says *what the framework correctly declines and
why*; it is **not** a derivation of any declined quantity and
**not** a licence to classify unsolved problems as "features."
The discriminator is the entire content: **no obstruction
exhibited ⇒ the item stays open.** Deriving a quantity the
principle declines (an anchor value, the ontological root) would
not vindicate the framework — it would *contradict* the relevant
forcing (a derived `v/M_P` would mean prime-5 is reachable; a
provable R1 would falsify ∅). The principle is a consistency
statement about the framework's honest boundary, nothing more.
