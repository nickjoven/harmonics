# Substrate prediction selection — opening doc

## Status

**Opens a thread.** Investigates whether a *unifying* selection
principle exists across the framework's substrate-primitive ratio
predictions — that is, whether the substrate's apparatus, beyond
deriving each prediction through its own derivation chain, also
admits a higher-order principle that explains *which* substrate-
primitive ratio each prediction lands on.

**No load-bearing claim is made by opening this thread.** The
framework's existing predictions stand on their own derivation
chains. Each prediction is closed via the mechanism cited in its
source doc (Farey partition for `Ω_Λ`, cube identity for
`(q_2, q_3)`, etc.). The selection thread asks an
*additional* question — whether these closures admit a unifying
account — without depending on or affecting any closure already
in place.

**Null result is a non-invasive outcome.** If the thread finds
that no unifying selection principle exists, the framework's
existing predictions remain valid; the thread's verdict would be
that selection happens per-prediction via individual derivation
chains, with no higher-order pattern across them. This is a
substantive methodological finding — equivalent to declaring
"selection is per-prediction structural" — and does not require
any revision to existing predictions.

Class: methodological exploration (Class 3, articulation).

## The question

The framework's structural predictions come out as specific ratios
of substrate primitives, derived through specific mechanisms:

| Prediction | Ratio | Substrate derivation mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| `Ω_Λ` | `13/19` | Farey partition + Z_2 rep theory (`omega_partition_combinatorial.md`, `farey_partition.md`) |
| `Ω_b` (two-component) | `13/264` | Cusp-1/2 ground state on X_0(6) (`omega_b_alpha_beta_closure.md`) |
| `(q_2, q_3)` | `(2, 3)` | Cube identity uniqueness (`mass_sector_closure.md`) |
| `q_2³ = q_2 + 2 q_3` | `8 = 2 + 6` | Cube identity (`mass_sector_closure.md`) |
| `k_lepton / k_quark` | `9/8` | Chirality cross-link (`mass_sector_closure.md`) |
| `MEDIANT` | `5 = q_2 + q_3` | Definition (`canonical_glossary.md`) |
| `K_lepton (= Koide)` | `2/3 = q_2 / q_3` (proposed) | Under derivation in Koide iterations 1–8 |

Each prediction lands on a *specific* substrate-primitive ratio
out of multiple combinatorially eligible candidates. For instance:

- `Ω_Λ = 13/19` rather than `5/19` or `1/19` (the partition gives
  the specific Farey-level position)
- `(q_2, q_3) = (2, 3)` rather than other small-prime pairs (the
  cube identity has a unique positive-integer solution)
- `K_lepton = q_2 / q_3 = 2/3` rather than `q_3 / (q_2 + q_3) = 3/5`,
  `q_2² / q_3² = 4/9`, etc. — all Cauchy-Schwarz-eligible (under
  derivation)

Each individual selection is closed by the prediction's own
derivation chain. The thread's question:

> **Does the framework's apparatus admit a unifying selection
> principle that accounts for substrate-primitive ratio selection
> across multiple predictions, or is selection per-prediction
> structural via separate derivation chains?**

Either outcome is acceptable. The question is methodological, not
prediction-closing.

## Possible outcomes

The thread can resolve in three ways:

### Outcome 1 — A unifying selection principle exists

A substrate-aligned methodological principle is found that
accounts for ratio selection across multiple framework predictions.
The principle might take a form like:

- "Substrate predictions land on the *minimal* Farey-level / cube-
  identity / combinatorial ratio compatible with their derivation
  chain's structural constraints"
- "Substrate predictions land on the *smallest* substrate-primitive
  ratio consistent with their derivation chain's bounds (e.g.,
  Cauchy-Schwarz, positivity, parity)"
- Some other unifying combinatorial criterion not yet articulated

If found, this would be a methodological principle peer to the
Basepoint Principle and Discrete Extension Principle. It would
provide a deployable framework discriminator for future
predictions and might tighten existing closures by exhibiting
their selection forcing.

**Class**: Class 3 methodological articulation (new meta-principle).

### Outcome 2 — Selection is per-prediction structural

Each prediction's selection follows from its own derivation
chain's specific structural constraints, with no higher-order
pattern across predictions. The thread's verdict: "selection is
correctly per-prediction; the framework's combinatorial machinery
selects different ratios in different domains because the
derivation chains impose different structural constraints."

This is *substantively informative*. It identifies that the
framework's predictive landscape is not a single ratio-selection
principle but a *collection* of derivation-chain-specific
selections. The Discrete Extension Principle's discriminator
discipline already operates per-prediction; outcome 2 would
generalize that observation to the selection question.

**Class**: Class 3 articulation (negative result that clarifies
the framework's methodological shape).

### Outcome 3 — Some predictions are genuinely under-determined

Within the substrate's apparatus, certain predictions' selection
among substrate-primitive ratios is not forced by any derivation
chain. For these, the prediction's specific value is empirically
observed but not substrate-derivable beyond Cauchy-Schwarz (or
analogous) bounds.

This would push the affected predictions toward Floor status (the
framework's "structural mechanism known, specific value not
forced") or toward Class-2 numerology. It would *not* invalidate
the predictions; it would refine their Class status.

**Class**: refinement of existing predictions' Class statuses
where applicable.

## What this thread does *not* do

**Does not affect the validity of any existing framework
prediction.** Each prediction's closure rests on its own derivation
chain. The selection thread asks a meta-question about whether
the closures admit a unifying account; it does not propose to
re-derive any closure from scratch.

**Does not depend on closure for Koide.** The Koide iteration
trajectory (iterations 1–8, with reading 1c remaining as the most
concrete substrate-derivation test) proceeds independently. The
selection thread may inform Koide's closure (by surfacing a
unifying principle) but is not a prerequisite for it.

**Does not propose a new substrate primitive.** Any methodological
principle found in this thread would be a discriminator-clause
principle (like Basepoint or Discrete Extension), not a new
inviolable.

**Does not commit to closing.** The thread may resolve in any of
the three outcomes above. The Discrete Extension Principle's
third row ("operationally open" or "structurally bounded") applies
to methodological principles as it does to derivations: if no
unifying principle is found despite careful search, outcome 2
("per-prediction structural") is a valid resolution.

## Methodological framing

The thread is *exploratory*. The work consists of:

1. Cataloging the substrate-primitive ratio predictions in the
   framework and their derivation mechanisms.
2. Examining each derivation mechanism for *what selects* the
   specific ratio (structural constraint, bound, minimality,
   etc.).
3. Checking whether the selection mechanisms across predictions
   share a higher-order structural feature.
4. Either articulating the unifying principle (outcome 1) or
   reporting per-prediction selection as the verdict (outcome 2),
   or identifying under-determined cases (outcome 3).

Honest scope: this is multi-iteration work with outcome a priori
uncertain. The thread may produce a Class-3 methodological doc
in any of the three outcomes, none of which damages the
framework.
