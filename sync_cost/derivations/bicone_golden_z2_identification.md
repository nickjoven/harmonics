# Bicone Z₂ ↔ golden-algebra Z₂ identification

## Status

**Bridge theorem articulated.** The substrate's bicone Z₂ rigidity
(inviolable #1 / #3) acts on cascade-dynamics-induced `ℚ[√5]`-valued
quantities as the Galois automorphism `σ : √5 ↦ −√5` (equivalently
`φ ↔ ψ`). The identification is *forced* by three already-installed
substrate facts plus a standard algebraic uniqueness:

1. The substrate has a non-trivial Z₂ rigidity (inviolables #1, #3).
2. Cascade dynamics generate `ℚ[√5]`-valued quantities
   (`minimum_alphabet.md` §3: `F_n = (φⁿ − ψⁿ)/√5`, the two-mode
   decomposition).
3. The natural-irrationals closure `{φ, π, e, √n}` (inviolable #8)
   includes `√5` in the substrate's algebraic vocabulary.
4. The unique non-trivial Z₂ acting on `ℚ[√5]` is the Galois
   automorphism `σ` (standard quadratic-extension Galois theory).

By uniqueness, the bicone Z₂'s action on `ℚ[√5]`-valued substrate
quantities *is* `σ`. The bridge closes by algebraic necessity, not
by new physical postulate.

No new primitive. The contribution is the explicit identification,
which extends the substrate's Q-mod-2 reading from *integer-winding
configurations* (where the kink-number reduction of
`q_mod2_conservation_theorem.md` Step 1 directly applies) to
*ℚ[√5]-winding configurations* (where the Galois reading is the
natural extension). This unblocks the joint-sector determination of
the K(t) cadence question (`q_mod2_mediant_projection.md` / PR #178)
by supplying a derived `Q_inflation` value where the kink-number
apparatus alone gives "not defined" (irrational winding violates
vacuum-set discreteness).

## The theorem

**Theorem (Bicone-golden Z₂ identification).** Let the substrate
support the bicone Z₂ rigidity of inviolable #1 / #3
(`q_mod2_conservation_theorem.md`, `wave_particle_substrate.md`), and
let cascade dynamics on the substrate generate `ℚ[√5]`-valued
quantities via the two-mode decomposition `F_n = (φⁿ − ψⁿ)/√5`
(`minimum_alphabet.md` §3). Then the bicone Z₂ generator, restricted
to the substrate's `ℚ[√5]`-valued subalgebra, is the unique
non-trivial automorphism of `ℚ[√5]`:

    σ : a + b√5 ↦ a − b√5       (equivalently σ : φ ↔ ψ).

Consequently, the bicone Q-mod-2 of a substrate quantity `q ∈ ℚ[√5]`
is determined by the Z₂-character of `q` under `σ`:

    Q(q) = 0    if   σ(q) = +q   (σ-invariant, "matter side")
    Q(q) = 1    if   σ(q) = −q   (σ-anti-invariant, "dark side")

(General `q ∈ ℚ[√5]` decomposes into σ-invariant + σ-anti-invariant
parts; the Q-mod-2 of a pure-eigenvector quantity is determined by
its σ-character.)

## Derivation

### Step 1 — The substrate has a non-trivial Z₂ rigidity

By inviolables #1 and #3 (`substrate_determinism.md` L166-176):

- #1: `Z₂` topological-charge conservation; the bicone's mod-2
  invariant under any local process
  (`q_mod2_conservation_theorem.md`).
- #3: Unitarity / information conservation; bicone Z₂ rigidity
  (`wave_particle_substrate.md`).

This is a single Z₂ group acting on the substrate's configurations,
non-trivial (otherwise `Q ∈ ℤ/2ℤ` would be the trivial group and
conservation would be vacuous).

### Step 2 — Cascade dynamics generate ℚ[√5]-valued quantities

`minimum_alphabet.md` §3 establishes the golden-polynomial reduction
of cascade dynamics. The two-mode decomposition

    F_n = (φⁿ − ψⁿ) / √5,        φ = (1+√5)/2,   ψ = (1−√5)/2

gives the Fibonacci numbers as `ℚ[√5]`-valued combinations of the
two cascade eigenmodes (the φ-mode being the attractor / "live" side,
the ψ-mode being the suppressed / "dark" side with `|ψ| < 1`). The
eigenvalue separation

    φ − ψ = √5

normalizes the modes and enters the substrate's algebraic vocabulary
explicitly. Inviolable #8 (natural-irrationals closure
`{φ, π, e, √n}`) acknowledges this: `√5` is in the substrate's
admissible-irrational set, not introduced by new primitive.

### Step 3 — ℚ[√5] has a unique non-trivial automorphism

Standard algebraic fact (Galois theory of quadratic extensions):

    Gal(ℚ(√5) / ℚ) = ⟨σ⟩ ≅ ℤ/2ℤ,    σ : √5 ↦ −√5.

`σ` is the **unique** non-identity field automorphism of `ℚ[√5]`
fixing `ℚ`. Any Z₂ group acting non-trivially on `ℚ[√5]` (i.e., by
field automorphisms, not by some ad-hoc external action) must be the
group generated by `σ`.

### Step 4 — The bicone Z₂ acts non-trivially on cascade ℚ[√5]-valued quantities

This is the substantive step. The bicone Z₂ is a substrate-level
group; cascade dynamics live on the substrate; cascade-induced
`ℚ[√5]`-valued quantities are substrate quantities. So the bicone Z₂
acts on these — by *some* group action. There are two possibilities:

(a) **Trivial action.** The bicone Z₂ fixes every cascade `ℚ[√5]`
    quantity. This would mean the cascade's `√5` structure carries no
    Q-mod-2 information — every cascade configuration has the same
    bicone parity. But the framework uses Q-mod-2 on
    cascade-sector configurations throughout
    (`master_cascade_identity.md`'s n-parity is the cascade's
    Q-mod-2 charge per station, used in
    `q_mod2_mediant_projection.md`'s cascade-side reading). A
    trivial action would void this usage. Excluded.

(b) **Non-trivial action.** The bicone Z₂ acts on `ℚ[√5]` by a
    non-identity automorphism. By Step 3, this automorphism is `σ`.

Therefore the bicone Z₂ restricts to `σ` on cascade-induced
`ℚ[√5]`-valued substrate quantities. QED.

## Q-mod-2 extension to ℚ[√5]-valued windings

The bridge supplies the missing piece of `q_mod2_mediant_projection.md`'s
Step 2: how to assign Q-mod-2 to windings that are not integers
(and therefore not directly subject to the sine-Gordon vacuum-set
discreteness of `q_mod2_conservation_theorem.md` Step 1).

For an integer winding `Q̃ ∈ ℤ`: `σ(Q̃) = Q̃` (σ is identity on ℚ);
the σ-character reading gives `Q = Q̃ mod 2`, which matches the
kink-number reduction. Consistency check: the bridge agrees with the
standard `Q mod 2` on integer windings.

For an `ℚ[√5]`-valued winding `q = a + b√5`:

- σ-invariant part: `a` (integer/rational)
- σ-anti-invariant part: `b√5`

The bicone Q-mod-2 is the Z₂-character of the *anti-invariant part*:
`Q = b mod 2` (loosely; the integer-part-of-`b` parity for rational
`b`). For `q = √5` itself (`a = 0`, `b = 1`): `Q = 1`.

This is the Q-extension that the inflation rhythm's `√5` winding
needed.

## What this says (and what it does not)

It **says**:

- The substrate-level Z₂ that conserves Q-mod-2 on integer-winding
  configurations is the *same group* (under cascade restriction) as
  the golden algebra's Galois Z₂.
- The Z₂ assignment to non-integer (`ℚ[√5]`-valued) windings is
  therefore forced — not chosen — by the substrate's algebraic
  structure.
- The K(t) joint-sector residual
  (`q_mod2_mediant_projection.md` Step 3's open piece) can now be
  computed: `Q_inflation(√5) = 1`, structurally derived.

It does **not** say:

- That `ℚ[√5]` is the *whole* algebraic structure cascade dynamics
  generate. Higher-order eigenvalue separations (e.g., Markov-spectrum
  or other algebraic numbers from deeper Stern-Brocot iterations) may
  generate larger field extensions; the bridge here addresses only
  the `√5`-piece relevant to the inflation-winding question. Larger
  extensions would have their own Galois groups, requiring their own
  identifications.
- That the bicone Z₂ acts *only* via `σ` on the substrate. The
  bicone Z₂ is the substrate-wide Z₂ rigidity; its restriction to
  `ℚ[√5]` is `σ`, but it acts on other substrate sub-structures
  (e.g., the integer-winding sine-Gordon configurations) by other
  natural Z₂ actions (the kink-number reduction). The identification
  is *restriction-specific*, not global.
- That this dissolves the kink-number apparatus. The kink-number
  reduction of `q_mod2_conservation_theorem.md` Step 1 remains the
  primary definition for integer-winding configurations; the bridge
  is an *extension* of that definition to the `√5`-irrational
  windings cascade dynamics produce.
- That `σ`-anti-invariant ↔ Q=1 is a postulate. It is the unique
  identification consistent with: (a) trivial action on `ℚ` agreeing
  with kink-number `Q = (integer) mod 2`; (b) Q being a Z₂-character
  (homomorphism to `ℤ/2ℤ`); (c) the σ-eigenvalue structure of
  `ℚ[√5]` under the Galois Z₂. Any other identification breaks one
  of those.

## Falsifiers

- **Cascade dynamics generate a `ℚ[α]`-field with `α ≠ √5`.** If a
  rigorous reading of the cascade's algebraic structure shows the
  field extension is *not* `ℚ[√5]` but some other quadratic field
  (or a higher-degree extension), the Galois group changes and the
  bridge gives a different action. The identification stated here
  depends on `minimum_alphabet.md` §3's `F_n = (φⁿ − ψⁿ)/√5`
  decomposition being the right reading of cascade dynamics.
- **The bicone Z₂ acts trivially on cascade `ℚ[√5]`-valued
  quantities.** Excluded above by the framework's pervasive use of
  Q-mod-2 on cascade configurations (`master_cascade_identity.md`
  n-parity, `q_mod2_mediant_projection.md`). A demonstration that
  the Q-mod-2 usage there can be reduced to integer-winding-only
  configurations (sidestepping the `ℚ[√5]` piece) would void
  step 4(b)'s argument; the trivial-action branch would become
  admissible.
- **The σ-character / kink-number agreement breaks at some
  integer-winding test case.** The bridge requires the two Q-readings
  to agree on `ℚ` (where they overlap). If a configuration with
  `q ∈ ℤ` is found whose kink-number-Q disagrees with its
  σ-character-Q (under any choice of σ-extension), the identification
  is malformed. The agreement on `ℚ` is automatic (σ is identity on
  rationals), so this falsifier is essentially trivial — but listed
  for completeness.
