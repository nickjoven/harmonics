# Lesson 0 — the proslambanomenos: the music you already know is the substrate

## What this is

The **front door** of the curriculum (Tier A — the on-ramp). The
three problem-lessons (`lesson_8_35_vs_13_19.md`,
`lesson_rotation_curves_a0.md`, `lesson_cosmological_constant.md`) are
all *applications*; a newcomer needs somewhere to start. This is it.

The move it teaches: **the small-denominator structure you already
feel — as musical consonance — is the same structure the framework
runs on.** You are not learning a new mathematics; you are learning
that a mathematics you've had in your ears since childhood reaches all
the way up to cosmology. Maximally familiar in, framework out.

No prerequisites. If you have ever heard an octave, you have the
entry ticket.

---

## Segment 1 — The ground tone (≈8 min)

Ancient Greek music theory built its scale — the *Greater Perfect
System* — downward from a foundation note added below everything
else: the **proslambanómenos**, "the one taken on," the acquired
ground tone. Everything in the system is reckoned as an interval
*above* it. No ground note, no system.

Pythagoras (by legend, at the monochord) found that the consonant
intervals are exactly the **simple length ratios** of a vibrating
string: halve it → the octave (`2:1`), take two-thirds → the fifth
(`3:2`), three-quarters → the fourth (`4:3`). Consonance *is*
small-integer ratio. This is the oldest quantitative law in science —
older than Euclid, older than the lever.

The lesson's promise: physics has a proslambanomenos too — a ground
tone the whole structure is reckoned from — and it's the same kind of
object the monochord already showed you.

---

## Segment 2 — What every string already does (≈10 min)

Pluck a string and you don't get one frequency — you get the whole
**overtone series**: `f, 2f, 3f, 4f, 5f, …`, integer multiples of the
fundamental (from `lesson` chain / the harmonics thread). The
intervals between consecutive overtones are the consonances, in order
of simplicity:

```
 1:2 octave   2:3 fifth   3:4 fourth   4:5 major third   5:6 minor third …
```

A string player can *isolate* any one of these by lightly touching a
node — a **touch harmonic**. Touch at the reduced fraction `p/q` of
the string and you sound the **q-th harmonic** (the denominator). The
set of all such touch points, for harmonics up to order `N`, is
exactly the **Farey sequence** `F_N` — the rationals laid out along
the string, simplest first. *You have been fingering the framework's
rational lattice your whole musical life.*

---

## Segment 3 — Consonance is stability (the bridge) (≈12 min)

Why do simple ratios sound *good*? The musician's word is consonance;
the framework's word is **stability** — and they are the same fact
seen from two sides.

- **Linear side (the string itself):** low harmonics are the loud,
  well-separated partials; high harmonics are faint and dense. Small
  denominators dominate by *audibility*.
- **Nonlinear side (two rhythms in contact — mode-locking):** when
  two oscillators couple, they lock to a ratio `p/q`, and the
  stability of that lock — the width of its **Arnold tongue** —
  scales like `(K/2)^q`: *widest for the smallest denominator,
  narrower for larger q* (`phenomenon_glossary.md`). Small
  denominators dominate by *robustness*.

The bridge: **consonance and lock-stability are the same
small-denominator preference**, and the object that organizes both is
the **mediant / Stern-Brocot tree** — the rule that inserts
`(a+c)/(b+d)`, the simplest fraction between two, and so lays out
"which interval is next-most-consonant" (`mediant_derivation.md`). The
circle of fifths, the order in which intervals stop sounding
consonant, and the order in which mode-locks lose stability are *the
same ordering*. Music theory has been doing the framework's
combinatorics, by ear, for three thousand years.

---

## Segment 4 — The comma: where the rationals don't quite close (≈8 min)

Stack twelve perfect fifths (`3:2`) and you expect to land seven
octaves higher. You don't — you miss, by the **Pythagorean comma**:

```
 (3/2)¹² / 2⁷  =  3¹² / 2¹⁹  =  531441 / 524288  ≈  1.0136   (~23 cents)
```

Every musician lives with this: no instrument can be perfectly in
tune in all keys at once. Equal temperament *smears* the comma —
flattening each fifth by ~2 cents so the error spreads evenly. The
piano in the room is, by construction, slightly out of tune
everywhere, on purpose.

This is the rational lattice **failing to close** — a fact you can
hear. **And here's the discipline, even in the on-ramp:** the
framework *notices* a near-coincidence near the comma
(`1/comma² ≈ K_Greene` to 0.17%) — and **files it under Class 2**:
recorded, but *no structural route*, not a prediction
(`framework_status.md:125`). The same discriminator from lesson 1
applies to the framework's own pretty musical near-match. The comma
is a real musical fact; the `K_Greene` resemblance is an honest
coincidence, shelved. (If lesson 1 isn't taught yet, this is a
preview of why; if it is, this is the callback.)

## Segment 5 — The universe in the key of 13/19 (≈10 min)

Now the payoff — the music goes all the way up. The framework reads
cosmology as a **round** (a musical canon, voices entering in
succession on the same melody; `cosmological_cycle.md:222-244`):

- The **first voice** — our universe — enters at coupling `K=1`,
  singing the full `F₆` melody: all **13** Farey modes.
- The **second voice** — the "gap-twin" living in the irrational gaps
  between our locked modes — enters offset by `1/φ`, the **golden
  ratio**: *the most irrational interval, the one that never
  consonates with the first voice* (its continued fraction `[1;1,1,…]`
  is the slowest to approximate, so it never mode-locks).
- The two voices overlap but **never lock** (the golden ratio
  prevents phase-locking), and the equilibrium of the round — the
  share of the "mode budget" held by the stable locked sector — is

```
 Ω_Λ = 13/19 ≈ 0.684      (the dark-energy fraction; Planck: 0.07σ)
```

So the chain is one unbroken musical line: **overtone series →
small-denominator consonance → mode-locking stability → the Farey/
Klein-bottle partition → the cosmological constant fraction.** When
the framework says "the universe is a two-voice round in the key of
13/19," it is not a metaphor dressed up — it is the same
small-denominator mathematics the monochord started, carried to the
top.

## Segment 6 — The move, and the honest boundary

**The on-ramp move:** you already hold the intuition (simple ratios =
consonance = stability); the framework is that intuition turned into
mechanism and carried up every scale. Start from what your ear knows.

**The honest boundary** (the recurring beat of every lesson):
- The bridge is **structural**, not poetic: the *same* small-
  denominator / Farey mathematics governs musical consonance and
  mode-locking stability. That part is real and load-bearing.
- The **"round" framing** is the *interpretive* layer over a Class-5
  result (`Ω_Λ = 13/19`); the music is how you *narrate* it, not an
  independent derivation.
- The comma↔`K_Greene` resemblance is **Class 2** — an honest
  coincidence, not a claim.
- The formal bridge between the musical and physical pictures
  (**Proof C: The Bridge**) lives in the external `proslambenomenos`
  repo, cited here as its home — not reproduced.

Name those four, and the on-ramp stays honest: it borrows the ear's
intuition without smuggling in claims the framework hasn't earned.

## Instructor notes

- **This is lesson 0** — teach it first. It converts "here is a
  strange framework" into "here is the thing your ear already knows,
  made precise." Every later lesson can point back to "remember the
  monochord."
- **Do it at an instrument if you can.** Sound the octave, the fifth,
  a touch harmonic; play twelve fifths and let the room hear the comma.
  Lived experience is the whole point of Tier A.
- **Plant the discriminator early** (Segment 4): even the music lesson
  shelves a pretty near-match. That seeds lesson 1 and inoculates
  against "it's all numerology."
- **End on 13/19** so the on-ramp visibly connects to the applications
  (it's the same number the discriminator lesson defends and the CC
  lesson approaches from the magnitude side).
