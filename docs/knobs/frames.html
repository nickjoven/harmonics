what "rational" means here

A rational ratio $p/q$ is a state where two oscillators rotate together in a fixed relationship. From the lab frame this looks like motion. From a frame rotating at the joint rate, both oscillators stand still — the picture is aligned, agreeing, harmonious. The English word "rational" carries all of these meanings, and they collapse to one substrate-level fact:

$$\text{rational} \iff \text{exists a rotating frame in which the configuration is stationary.}$$

An irrational ratio has no such frame. Spin the carousel however you want; you can hold one dot still, but never both. The drift is intrinsic to the relationship between the two oscillators, not removable by changing how you watch them.

decoherence is a perspective

The same configuration that looks coherent (still, locked, harmonious) from one frame can look decoherent (drifting, swirling) from another. Coherence is not an absolute property of the state — it is a relationship between the state and the frame.

This is why the framework's substrate is "discrete in the dynamics, continuous in the medium" (`dynamical_quantization.md`): the medium itself is a continuum of possible viewing rotations, and the discrete locked states are exactly those for which some viewing rotation holds the picture still. The "discreteness" lives in the alignment relation, not in pixels of spacetime.

the dynamics, with the frame subtracted

In the lab frame, the oscillators obey

$$\frac{d\varphi_1}{dt} = \omega_1 + K\sin(\varphi_2 - \varphi_1), \qquad \frac{d\varphi_2}{dt} = \omega_2 - K\sin(\varphi_2 - \varphi_1).$$

From a frame rotating at $\Omega_{\text{view}}$, the apparent positions are $\tilde\varphi_i = \varphi_i - \Omega_{\text{view}}\,t$, and the apparent angular velocities are

$$\frac{d\tilde\varphi_i}{dt} = \frac{d\varphi_i}{dt} - \Omega_{\text{view}}.$$

The relative phase $\Delta\varphi$ is invariant — what changes between frames is whether each individual oscillator looks stationary or drifting. The lock condition is unchanged ($|\omega_2 - \omega_1| \le 2K$); the carousel just changes what you see.

presets, in words

  • lab — $\Omega_{\text{view}} = 0$. Raw dynamics, both dots rotating.
  • rotate with ω₁ — $\Omega_{\text{view}} = \omega_1$. Dot 1 stands almost still; dot 2 shows the entire dynamics relative to it.
  • rotate with ω₂ — $\Omega_{\text{view}} = \omega_2$. Symmetric.
  • rotate with the lock — $\Omega_{\text{view}} = (\omega_1 + \omega_2)/2$. The candidate locking rate. If the system is locked, both dots stand still in this frame. If not, they drift symmetrically — the carousel can't fix what the coupling hasn't.
